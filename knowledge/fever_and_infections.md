# Fever and common infections (bukhar)
Keywords: fever, bukhar, बुखार, temperature, taap, malaria, dengue, typhoid, viral

## Fever: what to do at home
Fever means body temperature of about 38 C (100.4 F) or higher. Most fevers are caused by viral infections and settle in a few days.
Rest, drink plenty of fluids (water, lemon water, ORS, dal water), eat light food, wear light clothes, and wipe the body with a lukewarm damp cloth.
Paracetamol is commonly used for fever, but the dose depends on age and weight, so ask a doctor, ASHA worker or pharmacist. Do not give aspirin to children.
बुखार में आराम करें, खूब पानी और ORS पिएँ, हल्का खाना खाएँ। दवा की मात्रा डॉक्टर, ASHA दीदी या फार्मासिस्ट से पूछें।

## Fever: when to see a doctor
See a doctor if fever lasts more than 3 days, or if the patient is a baby under 3 months (any fever), a small child, a pregnant woman, an elderly person, or has diabetes, heart or kidney disease.
Go at once if there is a rash, stiff neck, severe headache, confusion, fits, breathing difficulty, repeated vomiting, or the person cannot drink fluids.
बुखार 3 दिन से ज़्यादा रहे, या बच्चा, गर्भवती महिला, बुज़ुर्ग हो, या गर्दन में अकड़न, दाने, साँस फूलना हो तो तुरंत डॉक्टर को दिखाएँ।

## Fever in monsoon: malaria, dengue, typhoid
In the rainy season, fever can be due to malaria, dengue or typhoid. These cannot be told apart at home; a blood test is needed. Government health centres offer free malaria tests.
Prevent mosquito breeding: do not let water collect in pots, tyres or coolers; use bed nets; wear full-sleeve clothes. Drink only boiled or filtered water.
Danger signs in dengue: severe stomach pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding from gums or nose, black stools, extreme weakness. Go to hospital immediately.
